Digital Safety & Privacy

Understand Your Digital Privacy

Every time we use a smartphone, social media platform, website, payment application or online service, we create and share digital information. Data privacy is about understanding what information is collected, why it is used, who may receive it, how it is protected and what choices we have as individuals.

01 · Foundation

What is Data Privacy?

Data privacy refers to the responsible handling of personal information. It involves transparency, appropriate use, security and giving people meaningful control over information associated with them.

🔐

Privacy

Privacy is about having appropriate control over information about yourself and deciding when and how it should be shared.

🛡️

Security

Security focuses on protecting information and systems from unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure, destruction or loss.

👁️

Transparency

People should be able to understand what information is being requested and the purpose for which it is being processed.

🎯

Purpose

Before sharing information, ask why it is required and whether the requested data is actually necessary for the service.

⚖️

Responsibility

Privacy is a shared responsibility. Users, organizations, platforms and service providers all have roles in protecting personal data.

🧠

Awareness

Awareness helps people recognize risky requests, suspicious links, unsafe permissions, scams and unnecessary data sharing.

02 · Why It Matters

Why Should College Students Care?

Students are active users of social media, online learning platforms, shopping websites, digital payments, gaming services and cloud applications. This creates a large digital footprint.

Your digital footprint

A digital footprint is the collection of information created by your online activities. It may include posts, comments, photographs, searches, purchases, account information, device information and interactions with websites and applications.

Some information is deliberately provided, while other information may be generated automatically by digital services.

Privacy can affect real life

Exposed personal information can contribute to identity misuse, targeted scams, account takeover, impersonation, unwanted profiling and financial fraud.

Good privacy practices reduce unnecessary exposure and make it harder for attackers to combine small pieces of information into a useful profile.

03 · Personal Information

What Counts as Personal Data?

Personal data can include information that identifies or relates to an individual. Different services may need different information, but users should understand what they are sharing.

01

Identity Information

Name, date of birth, photographs, identification details and similar information associated with a person.

02

Contact Information

Mobile number, email address, home address and other contact details.

03

Financial Information

Payment-related information and transaction details can be highly sensitive and should be protected.

04

Account Information

Usernames, account identifiers, authentication information and account activity.

05

Device & Online Data

Device identifiers, IP-related information, browser details, cookies and online activity may contribute to a digital profile.

06

Photos & Content

Photos, videos, documents, messages and other content can reveal personal or sensitive information.

04 · Data Lifecycle

What Happens to Your Data?

Personal information can move through several stages during its digital lifecycle. Understanding this process makes privacy decisions easier.

1

Collection

Information is provided or generated when a service is used.

2

Processing

Data may be organized, analyzed or used for a stated purpose.

3

Storage

Information may be stored on devices, servers or cloud infrastructure.

4

Sharing

Data may be shared with processors, service providers or other entities depending on the circumstances.

5

Deletion

Data may eventually be deleted or retained according to applicable requirements and purposes.

05 · Threat Landscape

Common Privacy & Cyber Threats

Many attacks do not require highly advanced hacking techniques. Attackers often exploit human trust, urgency, weak passwords and excessive information sharing.

🎣

Phishing

Fake messages, emails or websites designed to trick users into revealing passwords, financial information or other data.

🎭

Social Engineering

Attackers manipulate people using trust, fear, urgency, authority or curiosity instead of directly attacking technology.

🔑

Credential Theft

Stolen usernames, passwords, session information or authentication codes may allow attackers to access accounts.

💳

Financial Scams

Fake payment requests, impersonation, fraudulent shopping websites and other schemes may cause financial loss.

📱

Malicious Apps

Untrusted applications may request excessive permissions or contain malicious functionality.

🧬

Data Breaches

A breach can expose information held by an organization. Users should respond quickly when affected accounts are at risk.

🤖

AI-Enabled Scams

AI can make fake messages, voice content, images and impersonation attempts more convincing. Verify unexpected requests independently.

📡

Unsafe Networks

Untrusted networks and devices can create additional risks, particularly when users access sensitive accounts or information.

06 · Practical Protection

Data Privacy Rules Every Student Should Follow

Privacy protection does not require being a cybersecurity expert. Small habits can significantly reduce unnecessary exposure.

✓
Use strong and unique passwords for important accounts instead of reusing the same password.
✓
Enable two-factor or multi-factor authentication wherever it is available.
✓
Never share OTPs, passwords, PINs or authentication codes with callers or messages.
✓
Check application permissions and question permissions that are unnecessary for the app's purpose.
✓
Review social-media privacy settings and avoid publicly posting unnecessary personal information.
✓
Verify suspicious links and websites before entering passwords, payment information or identity details.
✓
Keep your operating system, browser and applications updated with security fixes.
✓
Download applications from trusted sources and avoid unknown files or suspicious software.
✓
Think before posting photographs, documents, locations, college IDs or other information online.
✓
Be suspicious of urgent requests involving money, passwords, OTPs, account suspension or prizes.

Remember: urgency is a common manipulation technique

If a message says “act immediately,” “your account will be blocked,” “send the OTP now,” or “pay immediately to receive the refund,” pause and independently verify the request through an official channel.

07 · Social Media

Think Before You Share

Social media can make personal information easier for strangers to collect and combine. Information that appears harmless individually can become useful when combined with other details.

Information attackers can learn

Public posts can reveal your name, college, daily routine, location, friends, interests, travel plans, family information, photographs and other contextual details.

Attackers may use these details to create convincing impersonation or social-engineering messages.

Safer sharing habits

Review your audience settings, remove unnecessary public information, avoid posting sensitive documents and think carefully before sharing real-time location or travel plans.

Remember that deleting a post does not guarantee that nobody has already copied, downloaded or captured it.

08 · Account Security

Passwords and Multi-Factor Authentication

Password reuse is dangerous because one compromised password can put multiple accounts at risk. Unique passwords and additional authentication factors reduce this risk.

🔐

Unique Passwords

Use a different password or passphrase for important services. A password manager can help generate and remember unique credentials.

📲

MFA / 2FA

Multi-factor authentication adds another verification step beyond the password and can reduce the impact of stolen passwords.

🚫

Never Share Codes

OTPs and authentication codes are security credentials. Treat unexpected requests for them as a serious warning sign.

09 · India

Data Protection in India

India has established a legal framework for digital personal data protection. Students should understand the basic concepts without assuming that every privacy issue is solved by legislation alone.

Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 establishes a framework for processing digital personal data in India. It uses concepts including the “Data Principal” for the individual to whom personal data relates and the “Data Fiduciary” for an entity that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data.

Notice & Consent

The framework includes requirements concerning notice and consent for applicable processing activities.

Access to Information

The Act provides Data Principals with specified rights relating to information about their personal data and its processing.

Correction & Erasure

The Act provides specified rights concerning correction and erasure of personal data, subject to applicable requirements.

Grievance Redressal

Data Principals have mechanisms under the framework for raising grievances concerning applicable processing activities.

Data Security

Data Fiduciaries have obligations concerning reasonable security safeguards and protection of personal data.

Children's Data

The framework includes additional requirements for processing the personal data of children.

Important legal note

The DPDP Act, 2023 and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 have an implementation and enforcement timeline. Specific obligations may apply at different stages. This awareness page is educational information, not legal advice.

10 · If Something Goes Wrong

What To Do After an Online Fraud

Acting quickly can help reduce further damage. Do not panic and do not continue communicating with the suspected scammer.

01

Stop

Stop responding to the suspicious person, website or message. Do not send additional money or credentials.

02

Secure

Change compromised passwords, enable MFA and secure affected accounts using official recovery channels.

03

Preserve

Keep screenshots, transaction details, messages, phone numbers, URLs and other relevant evidence.

04

Report

Report the incident through appropriate official channels and contact your bank or payment provider quickly when money is involved.

11 · Myths vs Facts

Common Privacy Misconceptions

Myth

“I have nothing important, so privacy does not matter to me.”

Fact

Personal information can be used for impersonation, scams, profiling and account attacks even when it does not appear valuable.

Myth

“If an app is available in an app store, every permission it requests must be safe.”

Fact

Review whether requested permissions make sense for the application's purpose.

Myth

“A bank employee can ask me for my OTP.”

Fact

Treat requests for OTPs, passwords and authentication codes as suspicious.

Myth

“My social-media account is private, so everything I post is completely safe.”

Fact

Privacy settings reduce exposure but do not eliminate screenshots, downloads, compromised accounts or accidental sharing.

Myth

“AI-generated messages always look fake.”

Fact

AI can make phishing and impersonation content increasingly convincing. Verify unexpected requests independently.

Myth

“A strong password is enough to protect every account.”

Fact

Unique passwords, MFA, software updates, phishing awareness and careful account recovery practices work together.

13 · Glossary

Important Privacy Terms

Personal Data Information relating to an identifiable individual.
Data Principal The individual to whom personal data relates under India's DPDP framework.
Data Fiduciary An entity that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data under the DPDP Act.
Phishing Deceptive communication designed to obtain information or cause a user to take an unsafe action.
MFA Multi-factor authentication uses more than one authentication factor.
OTP A one-time password or code used for authentication or verification.
Data Breach A security incident involving unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, loss or destruction of data.
Digital Footprint Information and traces generated through online activities and interactions.
Social Engineering Manipulating people into revealing information or performing an action that benefits an attacker.
